Every device that touches office data is a door

The baseline, control by control

  • Updates: security updates installed promptly, automatically where possible (Ben's laptop updates overnight).
  • Endpoint protection: antivirus or endpoint protection running and current; licences renewed before they lapse.
  • Full-disk encryption: a lost device does not mean lost data (Sol's field laptop is encrypted).
  • Screen lock: locks after a few idle minutes; a PIN or password reopens it.
  • Standard user account: daily work without administrator rights; only IT installs software.
  • Host firewall: the device's own firewall is on; it matters most on public Wi-Fi.
  • Removable media control: only approved, encrypted USB drives; unknown USB drives are blocked.
  • Secure configuration: unused features and accounts are turned off; IT's baseline for every new laptop.

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 8.1 (user endpoint devices), 8.7 (protection against malware), 7.9 (security of assets off-premises) and 6.7 (remote working).

TIME: 9 minutes.

SAY: every device that touches office data is a door. Keep each one updated, protected, encrypted and locked. The guide's baseline has eight controls; the memory phrase groups them: update, protect, encrypt, lock.

PREDICT (before each press): "Which of these ten will this layer catch?" Let the room guess the count, then press. Start with no layers: all ten land.

KEY POINTS: Update closes old weaknesses and turns unused features off. Protect is four controls: endpoint protection, the host firewall, a standard user account, and USB control. Encrypt is what turns "we lost the data" into "we lost a device". Lock stops the person at your desk when you stand up.

PLAIN WORDS: an endpoint is any device that connects to office systems: laptop, desktop, phone or tablet. A standard user account is a daily account without administrator rights: malware opened by mistake gets only your limited rights.

The padlock means private, not honest

The padlock (HTTPS) proves

  • The connection between your browser and that address is encrypted
  • The site holds a certificate issued for that address

It does not prove

  • Who runs the site, or that they are honest
  • That the address is the one you meant to visit

Your connection is not private

The browser could not confirm the site's identity.

Do not click past it, and do not sign in. On an office system, tell IT: the usual cause is an expired or wrongly set-up certificate, which IT must fix.

Criminals can get a certificate for an address they control. In June 2019 the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center warned that phishing sites use HTTPS and the padlock to look safe.

TIME: 4 minutes.

ASK FIRST: "Hands up if you were taught that the padlock means a site is safe." Most hands go up. That is what this slide corrects.

SAY: the padlock means private, not honest. It proves the connection to that address is encrypted, and that the site holds a certificate issued for that address. It does not prove who runs the site, that they are honest, or that the address is the one you meant. Criminals can get a certificate for an address they control; in June 2019 the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center warned that HTTPS and the padlock do not prove a site is legitimate, and that phishing sites were using them to look safe.

CERTIFICATE WARNINGS: a full-page warning such as "Your connection is not private" means the browser could not confirm the site's identity. Do not click past it and do not sign in. If it appears on an office system, tell IT: the usual cause is an expired or wrongly set-up certificate, which IT must fix rather than staff learning to ignore.

LINK: this morning, the same padlock meant TLS: private. Now we add: not honest.

PHP2Shell: two flaws, every PHP website

What PHP is

The programming language behind many websites and portals, including WordPress sites and many systems built by local developers.

What went wrong

Two critical flaws, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 (CVSS 9.2), let an attacker take over the web server without signing in.

Why it is urgent

Details leaked before the fix came out on 21 October 2026. The first attack attempt was seen on 17 October, four days earlier.

Fixed in PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35. PHP 8.1 and older get no fixes from the PHP project: upgrade, or get a backported fix from your vendor.

TIME: 2 minutes.

BEFORE EACH RUN: check php.net and CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog; versions and exploitation status change fast. On 22 October 2026 neither CVE was in the KEV catalog yet.
